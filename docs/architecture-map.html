00The thesis

We built a governance engine, not a quotation form.

The core is a pure function — (policy, quote, rep_history) → decision + explanation. Because policy is an argument rather than a hardcoded constant, we can re-run that function against a policy nobody has saved yet, across the whole open pipeline, and show the blast radius before committing. That is the Policy Simulator, and it is roughly forty lines only because of that one design decision.

Three claims, each provable with a single click:

ClaimProof on screen
The logic is real, not hardcodedPolicy Simulator re-routes 4 of 15 deals live
We check the pattern, not the worst lineQ-1039 has no line worse than 3 pts over and still flags
The portal is genuinely restrictedDevTools — the margin field does not exist in the response

01System map

Five layers. Dependencies point only downward — the engine imports nothing from the API. That is what lets the simulator call the scorer fifteen times in four milliseconds with a policy that does not exist in any database.

Why it is layered this way

Purity at Layer 1 is not stylistic tidiness — it is the product. If anyone adds a database read inside score_quote(), the Policy Simulator becomes impossible and the demo loses its centrepiece.

02The scoring engine

backend/engine/scoring.py · 548 lines

Plain English

The part that decides whether a discount needs approval, and explains why in a sentence a salesperson would accept.

Technical

score_quote(policy, quote, rep_history) → RiskResult. Pure module, no I/O. Same inputs always produce the same output, so it is safe to call in a loop against hypothetical policy.

The four terms

The score blends four orthogonal signals — orthogonal matters, because each one catches something none of the others can see.

TermPlain EnglishFormulaCatchesWeight
S Severity One line is way over the limit max(overi) The flagrant single breach 0.35
A Aggregate Lots of lines are slightly over, and it adds up Σ(overi × rev_weighti) The blended requirement 0.30
L Leakage How much money we actually gave away Σ leaked / gross_margin Absolute exposure, not just % 0.20
Z Behavioural This is unusual for this rep (x − median) / (1.4826 × MAD) Discount above a rep's own average 0.15 
score = 100 × ( 0.35·norm(S) + 0.30·norm(A) + 0.20·norm(L) + 0.15·norm(Z) )

caps:  S_cap = 20 pts   A_cap = 5 pts   Z maps [1σ, 3σ] → [0, 1]

Routing bands

These are rows in the policy table, not constants in code — which is precisely why the simulator can move them.

ScoreRoute
< 20AUTO  Auto-approve
20 – 60MANAGER  Sales Manager
≥ 60FINANCE  Sales Manager → Finance
any line ≥ 15 pts overFINANCE  Hard override, regardless of score

The effective ceiling

ceiling = min(tier_ceiling[tier], category_ceiling[category])

Plain English

A Gold customer is allowed 15%. But Services are capped at 10% because they are thin-margin. The stricter limit wins, so a Services line on a Gold order is governed at 10%.

Technical

Policy.ceiling_for() takes the minimum of the two ceilings. Category limits therefore only ever restrict below the tier allowance — they can never grant headroom above it.

A trap I hit while building this

Because the ceiling is a min(), setting Software to 20% while the top tier caps at 15% does nothing at all — the tier still binds. That is dead config, and an admin who writes it will believe a rule is active when it is not. So dead_config_warnings() detects it and surfaces it inline on the admin screen.

Why MAD instead of standard deviation

Plain English

We measure "unusual for this rep" against their median rather than their average, because averages get dragged around by exactly the outliers we are hunting.

Technical

Median absolute deviation, scaled by 1.4826 so it is comparable to σ for normal data. robust_z() returns None rather than a number when the history is too thin to be honest.

Why

One wild 30% quote inflates a rep's σ enough to make their next wild quote look ordinary. That is precisely the failure mode an anomaly detector must not have.

Honest degradation — demo this on purpose

When a rep has fewer than five prior orders, or gave the identical discount every time, we cannot compute a behavioural signal. So we drop the Z term, renormalise the surviving weights to sum to 1, and say so in the UI:

"Insufficient rep history (3 prior orders) — scoring on policy components only."

Why renormalise instead of just skipping

Dropping Z's 0.15 weight without redistributing it would make every score for a new rep silently 15% lower — meaning a brand-new rep could discount more freely than a veteran. That is a real governance bug, not a cosmetic one.

Show this deliberately on Q-1047 (rep S. Nair, 3 orders). A model that says out loud what it does not know reads as trustworthy.

Attribution is exact, not estimated

contributions = {k: 100 × weight[k] × term[k] for k in terms}
assert sum(contributions.values()) == score   # always
If asked "is this SHAP?"

"For an additive model, Shapley values reduce in closed form to each term's contribution — so we compute them exactly rather than sampling." That is true, and it is a strong answer.

03The two worked examples

Memorise both. The first proves we implemented the brief; the second proves we understood it.

Q-1042 · Acme Corp — the problem statement's own example

LineCategoryGivenAllowedOver
Laptop Pro 14Hardware12%15%0
Onsite Setup ServiceServices18%10%8 pts
Extended WarrantyHardware15%15%0

One Services line breaks its own stricter limit, so the whole quotation is flagged — even though the customer is Gold and 15% "sounds fine on paper".

Q-1039 · Beta Industries — the differentiator

Four lines, over by 3, 2, 3 and 2 points. Nothing dramatic anywhere. Every max()-based rule on earth auto-approves this quote. Ours flags it.

Demo instruction

Show Q-1039 for fifteen seconds immediately after Q-1042. It is the cleanest available proof that we implemented the brief's actual requirement rather than a threshold with extra steps.

coach() — the same engine, inverted

Instead of "why was I flagged", it answers "what would it take not to be". Output for Q-1042: "Drop Onsite Setup Service to 10% and this quote auto-approves."

Technical

The score is monotonic in any single line's discount, so we binary-search each line (24 iterations) for the highest discount that still reaches the target band, round down to a clean 0.5% step, then verify the rounded value.

Two design decisions worth being ready to defend:

  • It prefers lines that are actually over their ceiling. The naive "cheapest cut" answer suggested trimming a compliant Laptop line, because lowering the order-wide discount cheaply reduces the Z term. Technically valid, useless as advice — and a rep who follows it and gets flagged again stops trusting the tool.
  • Advice is capped at the ceiling. A small line can sit six points over policy and still land under the approval threshold once revenue-weighted. Coaching to that number means telling a rep "stay over policy, you'll slip through" — the exact behaviour this product exists to stop.

04The Policy Simulator — the moat

backend/api/services.py · simulate()

What the room sees

An admin drags the Services ceiling from 10% to 8%. Before saving anything, the open pipeline re-scores live — cards change colour, counters roll, and a headline appears.

What the code does

Clones the live policy with the proposed overrides, then scores every open quote twice — once against live, once against the clone — and classifies each as escalated, relaxed or unchanged.

proposed = live_policy.clone(**overrides)     # a COPY — nothing persisted
for q in all_quotes():
    before = score_quote(live_policy, q, history)
    after  = score_quote(proposed,    q, history)
    ...classify: escalated / relaxed / unchanged

"Re-routes 4 of 15 open deals · exposes ₹846 of leaking margin"

AUTO 7 → 4  ·  MANAGER 6 → 8  ·  FINANCE 2 → 3  ·  computed in 4 ms

Why this is the winning feature

Faking it is strictly harder than building it. A blast-radius preview requires the scorer to be genuinely re-invokable against uncommitted state — you cannot hardcode "three turn red" and survive a judge dragging the slider to an arbitrary value. And it costs almost nothing: the same function, in a loop.

Know this before you rehearse

Tightening a ceiling can only ever raise scores. A single downward drag therefore produces escalations only — never a green flip. To show two-way movement, drag the slider back; the reverse ripple is the cleanest possible non-hardcoded proof. Keep it in reserve for questions rather than spending it in the scripted fifteen seconds.

clone() is defensive, and there is a test asserting that mutating a simulated policy cannot corrupt the live one. Without it, a judge playing with the slider would silently poison the demo database.

05The recommender

backend/engine/recommender.py · 222 lines

Plain English

"73% of orders containing a laptop also contain a docking station" — learned from 120 historical orders, not typed into a lookup table.

Technical

Association-rule mining. A pair co-occurrence index is built once at startup, then support, confidence and lift are computed per candidate against everything already in the cart.

support(j)       = orders with j / all orders
confidence(C→j)  = orders with C and j / orders with C
lift(C→j)        = confidence / support(j)

Why lift is the number that matters

A docking station appearing in 40% of all orders is not interesting. It is interesting because it appears in 73% of orders that contain a laptop — lift 2.6×. Lift separates a real relationship from mere popularity.

Three filters, each defensible

FilterThresholdWhy it exists
Margin floor≥ 25%The brief explicitly requires it. Hard filter, applied before ranking
Minimum pair count≥ 5Standard apriori guard — a pair seen twice in 120 orders can show spectacular lift by accident
Minimum confidence≥ 15%Stops 7%-confidence coincidences reaching a rep's panel

I added the last two after watching 7% and 9%-confidence suggestions surface in testing. "Why is a 7%-confidence pairing being recommended to me?" is a fair hit, and now it cannot happen.

Ranking uses margin rate, not rupees

rank = lift × margin_pct × (1.15 if promoted else 1.0)

Why

Ranking by absolute margin made a ₹5,400 Support SLA outrank the laptop's actual companion products every single time. That collapses into "sorted by price with a lift column decorating it" — exactly how a sharp reviewer would characterise it. Using the rate keeps lift load-bearing. The absolute rupee figure is still what we display, because that is the number a rep acts on.

Small honesty that matters

The response carries a basis field — co-purchase, promoted or none — and the panel takes its label from it. Showing "Frequently bought together" above results derived from nothing but a promo flag is a small lie, and a reviewer who adds one obscure item to an empty cart will catch it.

06The portal boundary

The brief requires "a real, separate, restricted view, not just another internal screen with a different label". This is graded, and a judge will open DevTools.

Plain English

The customer's page is not the internal page with fields hidden. It is a different response, built from scratch, that never contained cost or margin in the first place.

Technical

The portal handler constructs a fresh dictionary field by field. It is not a filtered projection of the internal object, so there is no field to accidentally forget to strip.

EndpointCarries
/quotes/Q-1042cost · margin · risk_score · ceiling · over · rep · contributions
/portal/{token}name · qty · unit_price · discount_pct · line_total

Why structural rather than filtered

If we filtered a shared object, adding a field internally next week would silently leak it. Building a separate structure means a leak requires someone to deliberately type the field name into the portal handler.

The test asserts on the serialised bytes, so it catches a leak no matter how it arrived. It then confirms the internal endpoint does carry those fields — proving the difference is real redaction rather than an accident of this quote having no margin.

Rehearsed move · 10 seconds

DevTools → Network → the portal payload. "The margin field doesn't exist in this response. It isn't hidden by CSS — the serialiser can't emit it."

The negotiation loop

When a customer counters from the portal:

  • The quote enters NEGOTIATION — legal from both APPROVED and CONFIRMED
  • It is re-scored as if the counter were accepted
  • If the new band is not AUTO, it moves to PENDING_MANAGER automatically, with no rep action
A test caught this

My first version jumped straight to PENDING_MANAGER, skipping NEGOTIATION. Routing through it keeps the audit trail's ordering honest: the customer asked, then the system re-routed.

07State, audit and guardrails

backend/api/state.py · schemas.py

The state machine

DRAFT → PENDING_MANAGER → PENDING_FINANCE → APPROVED → CONFIRMED
                                    → FULFILLED → INVOICED → PAID
  ↕ NEGOTIATION (re-entrant)
  ↓ REJECTED / returned to DRAFT

Defined once, in one table, so the UI and the server cannot drift apart. Illegal transitions return 409 — never a 500, never a silent success — with the legal set attached:

{ "error": "illegal_transition", "current_state": "PAID",
  "attempted": "APPROVED", "allowed": [] }

Why this is the highest-stakes guardrail

A judge approving twice, or hitting browser-back, is the one failure that is unrecoverable mid-demo — it poisons every step that follows. A crashed narrator embarrasses you for ten seconds; corrupted state ends the run.

The UI derives button state from allowed_transitions, so illegal actions are disabled with a reason ("Already approved by M. Shah, Aug 21"). A disabled button that explains itself reads as rigour; a missing one reads as an unfinished screen.

The audit spine

deal_events is append-only — every mutation writes actor, role, event type, reason and timestamp. Three other features are projections of it, for free:

  • The approval history table
  • The real-time dashboard, via SSE fan-out
  • The stall detector — now − max(event.timestamp)

Why it had to be decided at hour zero

Choosing an append-only log on day one was free. Retrofitting one at hour twenty would have been impossible, and all three of those features would have had to be built separately.

Two smaller guardrails

  • Idempotency keys on approve, confirm and payment — replaying returns the identical response rather than double-writing. A double-click on a projector is a real hazard.
  • POST /admin/reset restores golden state in 0.19 ms. Rehearse the line: "Let me reset to a clean state — one click." That reads as production tooling; debugging on stage does not.

09Q&A drill

Is the score hardcoded?

"Rather than tell you — let me show you." Then run the simulator. Best possible outcome for this question.

Is this real machine learning?

A transparent additive model plus item-item association rules. We chose explainability deliberately — a manager has to justify a routing decision to a rep, and a black box cannot. Being additive, Shapley values reduce in closed form, so attribution is exact rather than estimated.

Click to reveal

Why not just use the worst line?

Because §10 of your own problem statement warns against exactly that. Three lines at 2, 3 and 2 points over pass every max() rule while giving away real margin — that is what our aggregate term catches.

How is the customer portal actually separate?

DevTools → Network → the portal payload. The margin field does not exist in this response; it is not hidden by CSS, the serialiser cannot emit it.

Click to reveal

What happens with a brand-new rep who has no history?

We drop the behavioural term and renormalise the remaining weights, and we say so in the UI. Renormalising matters — otherwise a new rep's scores would be silently lower and they could discount more freely than a veteran.

Click to reveal

Why MAD rather than standard deviation?

One outlier inflates σ enough to hide the next outlier — the exact failure an anomaly detector cannot have.

Click to reveal

Why is that particular item being recommended?

Point at the reason line on the card: confidence percentage, lift multiple, and margin, all visible. Plus we require a pair to have occurred at least five times, so we never surface a coincidence.

The data model is already isomorphic to the standard Sales, Inventory and Subscription object graph, so the next step is packaging the engine as a drop-in module that decorates the existing quotation object rather than replacing it. 10Real versus stubbed

Judges respect a clear answer far more than a vague one. GET /_status shows the live board at any moment.

StateEndpoints
REAL · 9 score · coach · recommend · policy get/put · policy/simulate · portal read · events/stream · admin/reset
STUB · 19 auth · products · quotes CRUD · submit · approvals · warehouses/split · subscriptions · invoices/payment · dashboard · reports
How to say it

"The intelligence layer is real today. The CRUD around it returns contract-shaped fixtures so the UI could be built in parallel — each one swaps to a real handler without changing a response shape."

The stubs are not dumb. submit routes off the real score, the dashboard's leakage figure is genuinely computed, and the state machine is fully enforced.

Known gaps — say these before a judge finds them

  • Frontend not started. Next: Quotation Builder, then the Policy Simulator screen.
  • In-memory state, not SQLite yet. Response shapes are frozen, so the swap is invisible to the UI.
  • Narrator is template-based, not LLM. Deliberate — the LLM sits behind a 3-second timeout as an upgrade, never a dependency. The template output is often better anyway.
  • Warehouse split is greedy, not yet the exact subset enumeration.
